UK Affluence Map

Relative affluence and modelled income across 43,064 small areas in Great Britain, built entirely from open government data.

Before you use the numbers

  • The income figures are modelled, not measured. No open source publishes individual income below parliamentary constituency, so the small-area figures are estimated from HMRC tax data and an affluence index. They are sound enough to rank neighbourhoods and size a catchment; they are not sound enough to quote as fact about any single area.
  • The affluence index is relative. It is a percentile rank against the rest of GB, not a score with units. Grey on the map means “typical for Britain”; red is below the national median and blue above.
  • Bank branches come from OpenStreetMap. Contributed data, not an authoritative register — good in towns, patchier in rural areas, and slow to reflect closures.
  • Catchments are straight-line radii, not drive times. A circle is a poor model of access where rivers and motorways get in the way.

Sources: ONS, HMRC, Ordnance Survey, HM Land Registry, Registers of Scotland, National Records of Scotland, DfT (NaPTAN) — all under the Open Government Licence v3.0. Bank branches and place labels © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.
